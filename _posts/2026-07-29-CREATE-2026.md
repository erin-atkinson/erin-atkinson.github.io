I visited the University of Waterloo for a summer school as part of the CREATE QCS training program. It was a week-long event with lectures, workshops and a project.

## Activities
Groups were tasked with creating a grant proposal. The workshops supplemented this project, and we each gave a presentation at the end. Since the time was so constrained, we went with the first workable idea we had and proposed an investigation into the interaction between _Sargassum_ rafts and submesoscale surface currents using satellite data. I think we were the only group that wasn't a machine learning project. 

It felt a bit strange that the groups were pitted against each other, but ours was selected as one of the winners. It was a bout of intense work but I'm glad that it paid off for us. The other students I talked to felt a little overworked. I think that was true for us, too. Fortunately, our group worked well together and it went smoothly.

Since I was the most familiar with the topic, I was responsible for writing and presenting most of the background. The presentation was on Friday, after a long week of waking up just a bit too early, doing just a bit too much every day and I felt like I stumbled a lot. It can't have been that bad though. I opened with a short prelude about how the Mexican navy was deployed to deal with this stuff washing up on beaches. I feel like I wouldn't have thought to do that if I hadn't been reading more recently, it feels good to have what feels like a tractable payoff from that.

## Poster
I presented a poster on the work I was doing. This was just adapted from my Liège presentation and made somewhat hastily in PowerPoint. I had some good discussions with other students. I've been feeling a bit unsure about the currently published work of this project and I'm excited to really try to get the diagnostics pinned down. My upcoming exchange should be a helpful change of perspective.

## Travel
