As part of the CREATE QCS training program, I visited McGill university for a short exchange with Professor David Straub

## Research
Given the timeframe, it was important to come up with something to do that was feasible. I chose to investigate the spectrum of the frontogenesis equation under different levels of vertical mixing. This was inspired by a [recent arXiv submission](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22970) which looked at energetics across-scales for a pair of frontogenesis models. This is quite a different approach, though. I will also be able to use these simulations to make figures in my thesis.

We spin up a mesoscale flow with a low-vertical-resolution simulation of a thermocline, then use this as initial conditions for a simulation of a submesoscale mixed layer with a variable amount of vertical mixing. As the submesoscale flow evolves, fronts get stronger, reach a maximum, then die down. I looked at spectra of the surface quantities, as well as terms in the frontogenesis equation for the near-surface buoyancy. Videos of flow potentials and strain tensor invariants are on my [YouTube](https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PLCAz6Tsb7u8s&si=gcMXgEjK1RoVOemq).

The results from the surface buoyancy spectra are cool and I think agree with my large-eddy simulations. Spectral slope of $-4$ when mixing is significant due to arrest at a bulk frontal deformation radius scale.

## Travel
The trip to Montreal was a fun experience. The place I was staying had an air of a correctional facility and was halfway up the mountain, which was a bit of a workout every day. The location of the university contrasted with Toronto quite a bit even if on paper they are quite similar. The McLennon building at UofT being basically right next to the Spadina/Dundas Chinatown and Kensington Market has spoiled me. McGill is in quite a bougie area by comparison. They do have better on-campus offerings though, ice cream at the engineering cafe was a highlight.

I spent a lot of free time in the pedestrianised areas of the city. The Mont Royale neighbourhood was really nice.

I'm happy to be back home. I've been doing a lot of travelling and presenting and it's nice to be back feeling like I'm making progress on my work.
